The story

I did not get into real estate to sell houses. I got into it because I was tired of watching people with real money get bad advice.

My clients tend to be people whose careers arrived faster than the infrastructure around them. A contract gets signed. A show gets picked up. A company sells. Suddenly there are advisors in every seat, and nobody in the room has actually done the thing being discussed.

Real estate is usually the largest single decision in that stack, and it is the one most often handed to whoever happened to be nearby. I built my practice to be the opposite of nearby. I am the person who knows the street, knows the seller, knows the builder, knows what the appraisal is going to say, and knows what a trade deadline does to your timeline.

That means saying things clients do not always want to hear. It means telling someone the house is wrong, or the timing is wrong, or the number is a story the listing agent is telling. It has cost me commissions. It has never cost me a client.

Over the course of my career I have closed more than $300 million in sales and held broker licenses in six states, which matters for a simple reason. When a client gets traded, signs elsewhere, or decides to put roots somewhere quieter, they do not get passed to a referral partner they have never met. They get me.

Along the way the work has been covered by The Hollywood Reporter, Variety, Robb Report, the Los Angeles Times and Inman. I mention it only because it answers a question every new client is too polite to ask.
